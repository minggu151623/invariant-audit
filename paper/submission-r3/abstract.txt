A plurality-based functional-dependency check judges a target value using rows that match its determinant values. When determinant cells can change, group membership changes as well. We study the minimum number of context-cell edits that reverses a fixed target's decision, protecting its entire row. For checks with a common dependent attribute and independent unit-cost categorical edits, we prove a row-action dominance reduction for making a rejected target accept. That decision problem is NP-complete when the schema varies, already for binary domains and singleton determinants, with keys either fixed or editable. For the opposite direction, we derive an exact formula: nonsingleton groups retain their ordinary vote-margin cost, and only singleton groups can make editable keys strictly reduce the global radius. Exhaustive finite checks and a bounded optimization comparison support the implementations. A reused-source study covers 18,734 target cells from Beers, Flights and Rayyan. Among 13,762 accepted targets, 1,133 fixed-key infinite radii become finite and one finite radius decreases. No strict reduction appears in 30 exact rejected-target pairs; two full models time out. Stability does not establish correctness: 1,493 accepted targets disagree with references despite radius at least two.
