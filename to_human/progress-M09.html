Why this research

Schema validity is only the surface of a structured record. A record can have the right fields and still violate identity, conservation, transition, or revision relations. InvariantAudit tests whether an invariant-aware validator (M1) improves on a schema-only baseline (B0), while making false-positive, false-negative, and confidence behavior explicit.

This presentation reports only verified E001/E002 evidence. It does not claim production performance, external validity, generalization, novelty, safety, or public-claim eligibility.

What the evidence says

1 · The anchor repeated

E001 and E002 both supported their predeclared M1-versus-B0 rule within finite synthetic designs. ΔFNR was exactly zero in both; E002's primary intervals were nondegenerate and the three co-primary specification-stress intervals passed.

2 · Cross-field checks matter

E001 no-semantic/no-state ablations each fell to BA 0.75 and FPR 0.5. E002's corresponding ablations each had BA 0.823529412 and FPR 0.352941176. This is a within-design mechanism inference.

3 · The gap is now outside the generator

E002 broadened synthetic heterogeneity and specification stress, but both oracles remain hand-specified and their cases are generated. The next important question is transfer to externally authored records.

Lessons and limitations

  • E002's first execution was invalid because a validator compared rounded metric cells instead of exact fractions; the corrected run passed after remediation. Exact arithmetic and independent validation are evidence gates.
  • Synthetic heterogeneity, bounded misspecification, and bounded multi-fault stress improved the design, but they do not substitute for externally authored records.
  • Natural invalid prevalence, source/schema drift, sequence-level behavior, and a less recipe-coupled oracle boundary remain open.
  • The public-claim gate remains closed: public_claim_eligibility=false.
